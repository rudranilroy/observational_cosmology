======================================================================
WEEK 7
Joint flat ΛCDM inference with Pantheon+SH0ES, Cosmic Chronometers,
and BAO
======================================================================

Scientific objective

The goal of Week 7 is to extend the joint late-time inference pipeline

developed in Week 6 by adding Baryon Acoustic Oscillation measurements.

The analysis must remain restricted to a spatially flat ΛCDM cosmology.

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- read and validate a BAO dataset,

- identify the BAO observable used in each data point,

- compute the relevant flat ΛCDM distance quantities,

- construct a Gaussian BAO likelihood,

- combine Pantheon+SH0ES, Cosmic Chronometers, and BAO into a single

  joint likelihood,

- perform Bayesian inference for the flat ΛCDM parameter vector,

- compare the posterior constraints obtained from:

      Pantheon+SH0ES only,

      Pantheon+SH0ES + CC,

      Pantheon+SH0ES + CC + BAO.

This week is not intended to introduce early-Universe physics or a

non-standard cosmological model. The focus is the correct statistical

and numerical combination of late-time geometrical and expansion-rate

observables within flat ΛCDM.

======================================================================
Conceptual background
======================================================================

In Week 6, the total likelihood was constructed as

    ln L_joint = ln L_SN + ln L_CC,

where the supernova likelihood constrains the luminosity-distance

relation and the Cosmic Chronometer likelihood constrains H(z) directly.

In Week 7, we add BAO data. BAO measurements provide geometrical

constraints involving cosmological distances relative to the sound

horizon at the baryon drag epoch, r_d. Depending on the dataset, BAO

measurements may be isotropic or anisotropic.

Common BAO observables include

    D_M(z) / r_d,

    D_H(z) / r_d,

    D_V(z) / r_d,

or sometimes equivalent combinations such as

    H(z) r_d.

Here,

    D_H(z) = c / H(z)

is the Hubble distance, and for a spatially flat Universe,

    D_M(z) = c ∫_0^z dz' / H(z')

is the transverse comoving distance.

The volume-averaged BAO distance is

    D_V(z) = [ z D_H(z) D_M^2(z) ]^{1/3}.

The total likelihood for Week 7 is therefore

    ln L_total = ln L_SN + ln L_CC + ln L_BAO,

assuming that the three datasets can be treated as statistically

independent.

======================================================================
Parameter vector
======================================================================

The parameter vector used in Week 7 is

    θ = (Ω_m, H_0, M_B, r_d).

Here:

- Ω_m is the present-day matter density fraction,

- H_0 is the Hubble constant,

- M_B is the absolute magnitude nuisance/calibration parameter entering

  the Pantheon+SH0ES likelihood,

- r_d is the sound horizon scale at the drag epoch.

The parameter r_d appears in the BAO likelihood but not in the

Pantheon+SH0ES or CC likelihoods.

In this hands-on exercise, r_d should be treated as a free parameter

unless the instructor explicitly decides to fix it. This is a clean

late-time geometrical approach, because the exercise does not require

students to compute r_d from early-Universe physics.

======================================================================
Tasks
======================================================================

1. Start from the Week 6 joint Pantheon+SH0ES + CC script.

2. Add a BAO data reader.

3. Inspect the BAO dataset and identify the observable type.

4. Implement D_H(z), D_M(z), and D_V(z) for flat ΛCDM.

5. Build a BAO theory function capable of handling different BAO

   observables.

6. Construct the BAO Gaussian likelihood.

7. Combine the SN, CC, and BAO likelihoods.

8. Run an MCMC analysis for

       θ = (Ω_m, H_0, M_B, r_d).

9. Produce posterior summaries and diagnostic plots.

10. Compare the Week 7 posterior with the Week 5 and Week 6 results.
