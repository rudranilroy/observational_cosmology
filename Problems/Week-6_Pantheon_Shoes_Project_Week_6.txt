======================================================================
WEEK 6
Joint flat ΛCDM inference with Pantheon+SH0ES and Cosmic Chronometers
======================================================================

Scientific objective

The goal of Week 6 is to extend the Bayesian inference pipeline developed in the previous weeks by combining the Pantheon+SH0ES supernova likelihood with an independent Cosmic Chronometer likelihood. The analysis must remain restricted to a spatially flat ΛCDM cosmology.

By the end of this week, you should be able to:

- read and validate a Cosmic Chronometer H(z) dataset,

- construct a Gaussian likelihood for direct H(z) measurements,

- combine it consistently with the Pantheon+SH0ES likelihood developed in Weeks 4 and 5,

- perform a joint MCMC inference for the flat ΛCDM parameters,

- compare the posterior constraints obtained from Pantheon+SH0ES alone and from Pantheon+SH0ES + Cosmic Chronometers.

This week is not intended to introduce a new cosmological model. The purpose is to understand how independent late-time probes can be combined within the same statistical framework.

Conceptual background

In the previous weeks, the analysis was based on the Pantheon+SH0ES supernova sample. The supernova likelihood constrains the relation between redshift and luminosity distance through the corrected apparent magnitudes and the absolute magnitude calibration parameter.

For the flat ΛCDM model, the dimensionless expansion function is

    E(z) = sqrt[Ω_m (1 + z)^3 + (1 - Ω_m)],

and the Hubble expansion rate is

    H(z) = H_0 E(z).

The Pantheon+SH0ES part of the analysis uses the theoretical prediction for the distance modulus, or equivalently for the corrected apparent magnitude,

    m_b,th = M_B + μ_LCDM(z; Ω_m, H_0),

together with the full covariance matrix of the supernova sample.

Cosmic Chronometers provide direct estimates of H(z) obtained from differential age measurements of passively evolving galaxies. The basic relation is

    H(z) = -1/(1+z) dz/dt.

Therefore, unlike supernovae, Cosmic Chronometers directly constrain the expansion rate in units of km s^{-1} Mpc^{-1}. Their inclusion is useful because they provide an independent late-time measurement of H(z), helping to constrain H_0 and Ω_m in a complementary way.

In this week, the total log-likelihood will be written as

    ln L_joint = ln L_SN + ln L_CC,

assuming statistical independence between the Pantheon+SH0ES supernova data and the Cosmic Chronometer data.

Parameter vector

The parameter vector remains

    θ = (Ω_m, H_0, M_B).

Here:

- Ω_m controls the matter density fraction today,

- H_0 sets the present-day expansion rate,

- M_B is the absolute magnitude nuisance/calibration parameter used in the Pantheon+SH0ES likelihood.

Cosmic Chronometers constrain only Ω_m and H_0 directly. They do not depend on M_B.
